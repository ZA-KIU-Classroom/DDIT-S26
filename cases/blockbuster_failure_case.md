# Case: Blockbuster Strategic Failure

## Source
Christensen, Clayton, *The Innovator's Dilemma*
Publicly available business reporting, 1997 to 2010

## Purpose
Use this case to identify the board-level constraint in your client's business model. Every incumbent in a disrupted industry has a version of Blockbuster's late fee revenue. Find it. Name it. Address it in your recommendation.

---

## The core argument

Blockbuster's failure was not a technology failure. It was a business model protection decision made rationally at the board level. The decision was economically correct in the short term and fatal in the long term. This is the Innovator's Dilemma operating at the highest governance level.

## Decision timeline

| Year | Decision | Rationale | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2000 | Board declines Netflix acquisition at $50M | Late fees are $800M/year. Any streaming strategy eliminates this line. The board cannot approve a strategy that removes 16% of revenue with no certain replacement. | The acquisition window closes. Netflix scales independently. |
| 2002 | Strategy: defend and extend | Peak dominance. No immediate competitive threat. Capital directed toward store expansion. | $800M invested in physical infrastructure that becomes a liability as digital adoption grows. |
| 2004 | Launches Blockbuster Online | Four years behind Netflix. Netflix has 3 million subscribers. | Enters streaming with a 9,000-store cost base that streaming revenue cannot cover. |
| 2007 | Internal review cannot proceed | Dropping late fees requires finding $800M elsewhere in one year. The organisation cannot solve this problem in the available time frame. | The business model constraint is now terminal. No viable transformation path exists that preserves board approval. |
| 2009 | Debt reaches $1 billion | Asset-heavy model cannot be restructured without liquidating the core business | Exit path is now structural bankruptcy. |
| 2010 | Chapter 11 bankruptcy | | Complete reversal of market position in 10 years from peak dominance. |

## Three failure mechanisms

### 1. Business model protection
The board optimised for today's profit at the cost of tomorrow's survival. This is not irrational. It is economically correct in the short term. Every board member who voted against the Netflix acquisition in 2000 made a defensible short-term decision. The cumulative effect of those decisions was fatal.

The question for your client: which revenue stream or cost structure makes your recommended transformation hardest to approve at board level? This is the business model constraint. Name it.

### 2. Channel conflict
Blockbuster's retail partners (mall operators, real estate companies) benefited from high-traffic physical locations. Any digital strategy bypassed them. The partner network pushed back against digital initiatives because digital threatened their revenue too.

The question for your client: who benefits from your client's current distribution model and would lose under your recommended strategy? These stakeholders will resist transformation at the board level unless your recommendation explicitly addresses their interests.

### 3. Capability mismatch
The skills that made Blockbuster dominant (real estate selection, retail operations, physical inventory management) were irrelevant to streaming. The very assets that created dominance in 2002 became liabilities by 2007.

The question for your client: which capabilities that made your client successful are irrelevant to the transformation you are recommending? How does your operating model plan account for acquiring or partnering for the missing capabilities?

---

## Christensen's Innovator's Dilemma applied

Blockbuster is a textbook example of the Innovator's Dilemma. Netflix entered at the bottom of the market with a subscription model for frequent renters. Blockbuster's best customers were infrequent renters who paid high late fees. Netflix's initial offering was worse for Blockbuster's most profitable customers. The threat was invisible until it was too late to respond.

Classification: this is low-end disruption. Netflix first captured customers Blockbuster was least motivated to fight for, then moved upmarket.
