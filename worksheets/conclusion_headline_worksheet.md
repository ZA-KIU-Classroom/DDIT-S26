# Worksheet: Conclusion Headlines

## Purpose

Every slide in your final presentation must have a conclusion headline, not a topic label. This worksheet trains the distinction. Complete it for your current draft deck before the dry run.

## The distinction

A topic label tells the audience what the slide is about.
A conclusion headline tells the audience what the slide concludes.

The difference is not about length or style. The difference is about whether the audience would know what the slide proves if they read only the headline.

---

## The three-element test

A strong conclusion headline contains:
1. A number or specific claim.
2. A named entity (your client, a competitor, a market segment, or a geography).
3. A strategic consequence.

Test: read the headline aloud. Would a board member who has not seen the slide know what conclusion they should draw?

## Part 2. Examples by section

Use these examples as calibration. Your headlines should be at this level of specificity.

### Industry analysis section examples

Weak: "Georgian Banking Industry Overview"
Strong: "Three Georgian fintech entrants have captured 28 percent of SME lending in 24 months, compressing incumbent net interest margins by an estimated 4 percentage points."

Weak: "AI in Banking"
Strong: "AI credit scoring has reduced underwriting cost at TBC by an estimated 30 percent, creating a structural cost disadvantage for mid-tier lenders who lack the data stack to match it."

### Strategic options section examples

Weak: "Strategic Options Analysis"
Strong: "Option 2 generates GEL 12 million NPV over five years at a 22-month payback, the strongest financial case of the three options and the only one that builds platform optionality."

Weak: "Recommendation"
Strong: "Option 2 is recommended because it produces the highest NPV, the shortest payback, and the only option that creates a defensible data asset as a platform barrier to entry."

### Financial section examples

Weak: "ROI Model"
Strong: "Base case payback is 22 months; downside scenario with 30 percent lower adoption extends payback to 31 months and remains positive against the cost of inaction."

Weak: "Sensitivity Analysis"
Strong: "The most sensitive assumption is user adoption rate: a 30 percent reduction in adoption extends payback by 9 months but does not change the recommendation under any scenario we modelled."

### Roadmap section examples

Weak: "18-Month Roadmap"
Strong: "H1 data governance and infrastructure investments in months 1 to 6 are the critical path: no H2 AI deployment initiative can begin without them."

Weak: "Implementation Plan"
Strong: "Change management at 25 percent of total transformation cost is the largest single line item and the most common source of adoption failure when underfunded."
