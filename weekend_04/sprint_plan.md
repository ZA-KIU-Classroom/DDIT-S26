## Purpose

This sprint closes the semester. The final presentations are Sunday 31 May. The
quality of those presentations is determined almost entirely by what each team
does in these six days. Saturday 30 May is a coaching and refinement day, not a
drafting day. Teams must arrive Saturday with a structurally complete deck and a
near-complete report.

## Sprint standard

A team is sprint-ready if, by Friday 30 May at 09:00:
- the final presentation deck is complete and pushed to GitHub,
- every slide has a conclusion headline,
- the executive summary states three conclusions, not three topics,
- the 18-month roadmap has all seven elements per initiative,
- the governance plan names an oversight body, states a review cadence, and defines a human override protocol per high-risk use case,
- the comprehensive report is complete enough that a reader unfamiliar with the project can follow the argument from problem to recommendation,
- the team dry run has been completed and three specific weaknesses have been identified and addressed.

## Dry run protocol

The dry run is a hard commitment. It must happen on Thursday 29 May.

Format:
- Full 30-minute timed presentation. Stop at 30 minutes regardless.
- 15 minutes of hard Q&A using the peer challenge format below.
- After Q&A: identify three specific weaknesses. Write them down. Assign each to an owner. Set a fix-by time before Saturday 10:00.

Peer challenge format for the dry run:
- Number challenge: "Your base case payback is X months. What happens to that number if [named assumption] is 30 percent lower than your base case?"
- Structural distinction challenge: "Option 2 and Option 1 appear to have the same value proposition. What is structurally different about how each generates revenue?"
- Recommendation challenge: "What is the single external condition that would cause you to recommend Option 1 instead of your recommended option? Be specific."
