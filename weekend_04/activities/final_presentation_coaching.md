## Per-team structure (40 minutes)

### Step 1: Presentation (10 minutes)

Present the executive summary and strategic options section only.

The executive summary must state:
- the three most important conclusions from the entire project,
- the recommendation in one sentence,
- the ROI headline.

If the executive summary describes the structure of the presentation instead of
stating conclusions, that is the first piece of feedback. Stop and note it before
continuing to strategic options.

Strategic options must show:
- three structurally distinct options with different value propositions,
- the four-criterion evaluation matrix,
- the recommended option with three supporting reasons.

### Step 2: Instructor feedback (10 minutes)

Feedback is structured against the four final exam criteria.

| Criterion | Weight | Feedback prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic insight and analytical rigor | 30% | Does the executive summary state conclusions or describe topics? Is the recommendation specific and defensible? Does every technology finding have a strategic implication? |
| Quality and feasibility of transformation roadmap | 30% | Does the roadmap appear in the strategic options section logically? Are options connected to different roadmap paths? Is sequencing credible? |
| Executive communication and storytelling | 25% | Is the Pyramid Principle applied? Does the narrative move from problem to recommendation without detours? Are conclusion headlines on every slide? |
| Professional presentation standards and polish | 15% | Is the deck visually consistent? Is timing under control? Does the team present with confidence and composure? |

Instructor gives one specific commendation and one specific improvement per
criterion. Four commendations and four improvements total. Vague feedback such as
"good analysis" or "needs more depth" is not used.

### Step 3: Peer team challenge (10 minutes)

The peer team assigned to this slot delivers two challenges. The instructor calls
on the peer team by name. No open mic.

**Challenge 1: Number challenge**

Format: "Your base case payback is [X] months. Your [named assumption] is [A].
What happens to payback if [named assumption] is 30 percent lower than your base case?"

The presenting team must answer with a specific number or range, not a narrative.
If they cannot answer, that is a gap in the ROI model to fix before Sunday.

**Challenge 2: Structural distinction challenge**

Format: "Option [X] and Option [Y] appear to have the same value proposition.
What is structurally different about how each generates revenue?"

The presenting team must name a specific structural difference. If they cannot,
the options are not yet distinct enough. That is the fix for tonight.

### Step 4: Three improvements (10 minutes)

The presenting team has 10 minutes to write their three specific improvements.
These are recorded in the team GitHub repo as `weekend4_coaching_notes.md`.

The three improvements must be:
- specific enough that a teammate can implement them without further discussion,
- assigned to a named owner,
- completable before Sunday 10:00.

Vague improvements such as "strengthen the analysis" or "improve the slides"
are not acceptable. Specific improvements look like:
- "Add a sensitivity table to slide 14 showing payback at 70 percent of base case revenue. Owner: [name]. Done by: Saturday 22:00."
- "Rewrite the executive summary. Three conclusion sentences, not topic labels. Owner: [name]. Done by: Saturday 21:00."
- "Restructure Option 2 to show a different revenue model from Option 1. Add one sentence explaining the structural difference. Owner: [name]. Done by: Saturday 23:00."

## Common gaps to watch for

These are the most consistent weaknesses from the Weekend 3 midterm presentations.
The instructor watches for these specifically.

**Executive summary**
The executive summary describes the presentation structure instead of stating
conclusions. Fix: write the three most important conclusions the board needs to
act on. If you cannot write them in three sentences, the analysis is not complete.

**Strategic options**
Options are differentiated by budget or scale, not by value proposition or revenue
model. Fix: apply the structural distinction test. Different value proposition,
different revenue model, different capability requirement. If two options share the
same value proposition, they are not two strategic options.

**ROI model**
The sensitivity analysis shows optimistic and base case only. The board constructs
the downside in Q&A. You want to show it first. Fix: add a third scenario showing
the worst credible payback period with named assumptions.

**Roadmap**
Data governance does not appear in months 1 to 6. AI deployment initiatives appear
before the prerequisites are in place. Fix: move data governance and oversight body
establishment to months 1 to 3. No AI deployment initiative can precede them.

**Change management**
Change management appears as a bullet in the risk register, not as a workstream.
Fix: move it to the roadmap as a named initiative with a budget estimate at 20 to
30 percent of total transformation cost and a named owner function.

**Governance plan**
Governance is described as a principle rather than a structure. Fix: name the
oversight body, define its membership and decision rights, state the review cadence,
and write the human override protocol per high-risk use case.
