Sunday 31 May 2026
Final Exam: 40 points
Delivery: On campus (in person)

## Read this before Sunday 10:00

These rules are fixed. They are read aloud once at 10:00. Questions about the rules
are addressed before 10:15. After 10:15 the boardroom is in session and the rules
apply without exception.

---

## Room configuration

Presenting team: seated at the front, laptop connected to the projector.
Peer panel: two non-presenting teams seated as the board panel.
Instructor: seated with the panel.

Arrive by 09:45. Connect the laptop to the projector and confirm the connection
before 10:00. Do not use the setup time after 10:00. If there is a technical
problem, the presenting team's time still starts at 10:15.

## Presenting team rules

**Time limit: 30 minutes. Enforced without exception.**

The instructor stops the presentation at 30 minutes regardless of where the team
is in the deck. A team that has not reached the recommendation by minute 25 has
made a structural error. Practice the timing before Sunday.

**Recommendation first.**

The executive summary appears on slide 1. The board sees the recommendation before
the evidence. A presentation that builds to the recommendation at the end fails the
Pyramid Principle criterion.

**All four team members must speak.**

Each member presents at least one full section. A team where one member presents
90 percent of the deck loses points on professional presentation standards.

**No reading from slides.**

Slides carry conclusion headlines and key evidence. The presenter carries the
explanation and the analytical reasoning. Reading slide text verbatim is not a
boardroom presentation.

## Q&A rules

**Time: 15 minutes. Starts immediately when the presentation ends.**

The Q&A timer is independent. It does not compensate for presentation time used.

**Peer panel leads Q&A.**

The instructor supplements after the peer panel has asked their questions. The
peer panel is not a warm-up for the instructor. Peer challenges must come first.

**Required challenges.**

Each peer team must ask at minimum:
- one number challenge, and
- one structural distinction challenge.

Clarifying questions do not count toward the challenge requirement.

**Challenge formats.**

Number challenge:
"Your base case payback is [X] months. Your [named assumption] is [A]. What happens
to payback if [named assumption] is 30 percent lower than your base case?"

Structural distinction challenge:
"Option [X] and Option [Y] appear to have the same value proposition. What is
structurally different about how each generates revenue?"

Recommendation challenge (optional third challenge):
"What is the single external condition that would cause you to recommend Option [X]
instead of your recommended option? Be specific — not general market uncertainty."

Implementation challenge (optional fourth challenge):
"Your roadmap shows [initiative A] in month 3 and [initiative B] in month 4. What
specifically is completed in month 3 that makes month 4 viable?"

**Presenting team Q&A conduct.**

Answer directly. State a number, a name, or a specific condition. Do not restate
the question. Do not give a narrative when a number is asked for. If the team does
not know the answer, say so and state what additional analysis would resolve it.
That is a more credible answer than a wrong number.

## Peer scoring

Each peer team is assigned one of the four final exam criteria before presentations
begin. Assignments are announced at 10:00.

Peer scoring sheets are printed and distributed at the start of the session.
Complete the sheet during and immediately after each presentation. Submit the sheet
to the instructor before the break following that presentation.

Scores are not shared between teams until all three teams have presented.
Peer scores are averaged with the instructor score. Peer scores count toward
the final grade.

### Scoring criteria reminder

| Criterion | Weight | What to score |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic insight and analytical rigor | 30% | Are conclusions specific and defensible? Does every technology finding have a strategic implication? Is the recommendation supported by evidence? |
| Quality and feasibility of transformation roadmap | 30% | Does the roadmap have all seven elements per initiative? Is sequencing credible? Is data governance before AI deployment? Is change management a named workstream? |
| Executive communication and storytelling | 25% | Is the Pyramid Principle applied? Conclusion headlines on every slide? Does the narrative move cleanly from problem to recommendation? |
| Professional presentation standards and polish | 15% | Timing controlled? All members present confidently? No reading from slides? Visual consistency throughout? |
