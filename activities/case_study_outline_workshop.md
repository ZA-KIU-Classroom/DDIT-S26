# Activity: Individual Case Study Outline

## Purpose

Select a case and produce a structured outline before leaving Sunday. An unselected case by Sunday evening loses a week of the available sprint time.

## Duration
25 minutes

## Session
Sunday Block 4, 15:22 to 15:50

## Output
`case_study_outline_v1.md` in your individual folder

---

## What the case study analysis requires

The individual case study analysis is 20 points, individual submission, 10 to 12 pages. Apply the full course framework stack to a real organisation's digital transformation. Submitted independently.

It is not a company summary. It is a framework-driven analysis of a strategic decision. Every framework application ends with a conclusion in the format: "This means that..."

---

## Instructions

### Step 1. Select your case (8 minutes)

Name the organisation and the specific digital transformation decision you will analyse.

Criteria for a good case:
- The organisation made a documented AI or digital transformation decision in the last five years.
- At least three of the four course frameworks can be applied with available public information.
- The outcome is knowable: the transformation succeeded, failed, or is in progress with measurable indicators.

Avoid:
- Cases with only press release coverage and no analytical depth.
- Cases where the decision has not yet been made.
- Cases covered extensively in prior academic work without a new angle.

Strong case examples:
- TBC Bank digital transformation (Georgian banking context, rich public data)
- Klarna AI credit underwriting pivot
- Amazon logistics AI transformation
- NHS Digital AI deployment failure
- Kodak: only if your analysis adds a specific angle not covered in class

### Step 2. Framework fit check (7 minutes)

Do not write "Analyse using Rogers." Write: "How did this organisation's data infrastructure sequencing compare to the Rogers prerequisite model, and what was the consequence of getting that sequence wrong?"

## Quality check

Before submitting:
- Organisation named and decision specified, not just the industry.
- Each framework has a specific answerable question, not a category label.
- Section 3, 4, and 5 headlines contain a number, a named entity, and a strategic consequence.
- At least three primary sources confirmed.
- One data gap identified with a plan to fill it.
